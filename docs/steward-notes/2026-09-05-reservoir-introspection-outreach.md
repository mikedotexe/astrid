cognitive phenomena including introspection. His 2025 introspection work and

a particularly relevant critic of our distinction between post-forward logit

[personal research page](https://jlindsey15.github.io/),
[2025 introspection paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html),
[2026 workspace paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html).

**Public route:** `jacklindsey@anthropic.com`, explicitly published as the
corresponding-author address on the 2026 paper. Use one address, not several.

**Our proposed ask:** Is the pre/post-processor invariance check followed by an
uncoupled text-observer control a sound starting point? What additional test
would justify a claim about the assembled system's self-monitoring, and what
would still be missing for model-internal recognition?

**Material:** Begin with the brief. Offer full-draft sections 1, 4, 5, 9, and 10
and, after permission review, a minimal numerical example. Do not attach a
private transcript collection or request access to internal Anthropic tools.

**Expectation:** A correction, relevant reference, or referral would be a
valuable outcome. No response would not be a scientific verdict.

### To Jack Lindsey

**Subject:** A control-design question about reservoir-coupled self-reports

Hi Jack,

I'm developing Astrid and Minime, persistent agent systems with recurrent
reservoirs and language-model interfaces, through a human-led, AI-assisted
engineering collaboration. Their first-person reports prompted source
investigations that exposed a logit-narrowing bug and misleading context
interfaces. A small offline replay confirms changes in token probabilities,
not introspective recognition.

Your introspection work makes me think you could help sharpen one specific
question. In our inspected path, reservoir coupling acts after the transformer
forward pass. How should we distinguish downstream steering from useful
self-monitoring by the assembled system? We're considering matched-prefix
pre/post-processor checks and an uncoupled observer given the same generated
text, but would welcome a missing control or a better starting point.

I have a short brief and a fuller draft that disclose the prompt confounds,
inconclusive continuations, and deployment limits. May I send the brief?
A brief correction or reference would already be very helpful. We're not
seeking an endorsement or a consciousness verdict.

Thank you,
Mike

### To Mantas Lukoševičius

**Subject:** ESN feedback and state-contrast review for a small LLM coupling study

Dear Dr. Lukoševičius,

I'm working on persistent agents whose recurrent readouts modify language-model
logits, with generated-token embeddings feeding back into the reservoir. Close
reading of their reports led us to reproduce and repair a lower-tail logit
transformation that violated common-offset invariance.

We then ran a small copied-state replay: two nearby reservoir states crossed
with two contexts, first under a common supplied prefix. It established a
probability effect, but is far too narrow to establish robust state recognition.

Given your practical ESN work, I'd value guidance on the next design: which
state contrasts and feedback controls would distinguish meaningful dynamical
dependence from a poorly calibrated readout? We would particularly welcome a
critique of timescale separation, washout, and feedback-delay handling.

May I send a brief architecture and results note? We can discuss the numerical
case without sharing agent journals. This is a request for mechanical and
experimental criticism, not validation of a claim about reservoir experience.

Thank you,
Mike

## Sequence and Boundaries

1. Mike reviews the brief and proposed recipients. Keep this internal strategy
   separate from the document a recipient sees.
2. Confirm the published route and any intake requirements at send time. For
   Eleos, inspect the designated form and applicable privacy information before
   submitting the excerpt-free brief. Selective sharing is not a confidentiality
   guarantee; an organization may retain or route a submission internally.
3. Send one tailored, bounded inquiry per first-wave group, only after Mike
   authorizes outreach. No CC list, guessed relationship, or unsolicited archive.
4. Record any actual reply separately from our interpretation of it. Obtain
   permission before quoting a private reply, naming someone as a reviewer, or
   describing their involvement publicly.
5. Incorporate substantive criticism, then approach second-wave reviewers with
   the question relevant to their work. Do not turn non-response into a cascade
   of messages to colleagues at the same institution.

The sensible first success is **a better experiment**, not a prominent person
saying the accounts are extraordinary. None of this plan resumes the paused
automation, changes live controls, starts a study, or grants publication consent.
